# Weekly article and LinkedIn post template

Write on Sunday after the review. Target: 30 to 40 minutes, from a draft you then rewrite in your own voice.

---

## 1. Hashnode article (800 to 1200 words)

**Title:** Week N: <one concrete takeaway, not a topic name>

1. **Hook (2 to 3 sentences).** One real thing from this week that surprised you or failed. A specific moment, not a summary.
2. **The idea, in plain language.** Explain it to an RPA developer: what problem does it solve?
3. **Show it running.** A short code excerpt plus real output or a screenshot from your own machine.
4. **The UiPath bridge, and where it breaks.** Keep the "where it breaks" part; it is what makes the post credible.
5. **What I got wrong this week.** At least one real mistake or misconception you fixed.
6. **Try it yourself.** Link to the folder in the repo.
7. **Next week.** One line.

## 2. LinkedIn post (about 150 words, native, no link in the body)

- Line 1: the hook from the article, standing on its own.
- Two or three short paragraphs: the insight, the UiPath bridge, the mistake.
- End with one real question to RPA / AI people.
- No external link in the body. Put it in the first comment.
- Hashtags: at most 3.

## 3. First comment

> Full write-up with code: <Hashnode link>  
> Repo (exercises and the RPA↔AI Rosetta Stone): <GitHub link>

## 4. Before you post

- [ ] At least one screenshot or output from my own run
- [ ] At least one mistake or surprise written in my own words
- [ ] The analogy states where it breaks
- [ ] Repo folder is pushed and the link works
- [ ] No API keys or private data in any screenshot
- [ ] Tagged only people the post is genuinely about (zero is fine)

## 5. After posting

Spend 10 minutes, two or three times this week, leaving substantive comments on other people's UiPath and AI-agent posts. That does more for network growth than the post itself.
